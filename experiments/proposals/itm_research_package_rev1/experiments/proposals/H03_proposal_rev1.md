# H03 | Time variability across a single mountain ridge

**Decision.** Test whether the distribution of hourly median loss narrows on a path whose two terminals see the same isolated crest. Start from `000-baseline` at `5d39122275ef2b1b9c93be1e72007e61a90b53cc`. This is a shape experiment with zero fitted parameters. Its primary outcome is the error in the measured 10–90% loss span on eligible long-term paths. It predicts identical 50% loss on every Phase 2 site and on every censored row.

## Mechanism and evidence

A ridge visible from both terminals can support diffraction over two unobstructed legs. Atmospheric variability on the two legs can differ from that on a scatter path of the same total length. Barsis and Kirby observed a narrower long-term fading range on a 223 km Pikes Peak path at 100 and 751 MHz than expected for comparable scatter paths. Barsis extended the comparison to three paths in Colorado, Alaska, and Europe at 750–900 MHz. TN 101 treats the edge as a common terminal and convolves the two leg distributions under independence. Here the conjecture is that a terrain-derived implementation of that mechanism adds information beyond ITM's whole-path effective distance and R02's single spread multiplier. Those early measurements may overlap the ancestry of the development data. They establish physical precedent. ## Geometry and implementation

1. Add an opt-in variability switch to `itm_variant/params_exp.m`. Use the point-to-point profile and the existing effective Earth radius in `itm_p2p_core_exp.m`. Identify the common interior sample from the two raw `find_horizons` maxima **before** `quick_pfl` can replace them with smooth-earth horizons. A tie, different horizons, or either leg lacking an interior sample gives stock ITM. Apply the change to broadcast time variability. Preserve the existing behavior for area mode and the other variability modes.
2. Put the profile in a common chord frame. At displacement $x$, use $z^*(x)=z(x)+x(D-x)/(2a_e)$, with all lengths in metres. Add structural heights at the original endpoints. Form straight rays from each endpoint to the common crest at terrain height. For every interior sample in each leg compute clearance (ray elevation minus $z^*$) divided by $r_1=\sqrt{\lambda t(\ell-t)/\ell}$, where $t$ and $\ell$ are distance along and length of that leg. Let $u$ be the minimum of those clearances and the bare-terrain crest height above the uncurved terminal chord divided by the full-link $r_1$ at the crest. Define $w=u_+^2/(1+u_+^2)$, $u_+=\max(0,u)$. This proposed taper suppresses Earth-curvature-only blockage and goes to zero when a competing terrain sample grazes a leg ray. It can also suppress broad summits. Record its distribution before examining errors.
3. For each leg, fit a straight line to all equally spaced bare-terrain samples by least squares. At each end take $h_e=h_s+\max(0,z_{end}-z_{fit})$. Use the physical antenna height $h_s$ at the outer terminal and zero at the crest. The tip height enters the variability geometry alone. Using every sample is a reproducible approximation to TN 101's visible-terrain fit in §5.2.1. Feed the resulting heights, leg length, fixed frequency, and original climate into the stock effective-distance and time-curve calculations inside `variability`. Keep the same refractivity and original profile spacing.
4. Write $g_j(Z)=Y_{T,j}$ for leg $j$'s stock time-deviate curve with a standard normal $Z$. The hourly loss offset has distribution $B_j=-g_j(Z)$. Compute the quantile $Q(p)$ of $B_1+B_2$ under independent normals, $p$ a fraction. An implementation can invert the monotone piecewise-linear $g_j$ and integrate the conditional normal CDF over the other leg. Use adaptive quadrature, bracketed inversion, and the survival function in the upper tail. Verify convergence under tighter tolerance and terminal reversal. No Monte Carlo seed is required.
5. Before ITM computes its situation coupling and negative-attenuation compression, replace $Y_T(p)$ by $Y_T(.5)+(1-w)[Y_T(p)-Y_T(.5)]-w[Q(p)-Q(.5)]$. Use the existing ITM value at 50%, including its deviate convention. Center the **convolved** quantile once. Asymmetric independent distributions can have a median of the sum different from the sum of the medians. Retain the original variability median, location and situation terms, and final attenuation compression. Preserve scalar and vector percentile interfaces.

## Falsification and decision

- Before looking at outcomes, report unique long-term paths, receiver groups and $w$ distribution, including counts for $0<w<0.5$ and $w\geq0.5$. Predeclare the latter as the primary stratum. On it, compare paired absolute errors in $L_{90}-L_{10}$ for the candidate, stock and R02. For each comparator define gain as its absolute span error minus the trial's. Average repeats within physical path, use paired resampling of paths within receiver group, and report every group and leave-one-group-out estimate. Before a transfer claim, require at least two receiver groups with five eligible physical paths each, positive gain in both, and a pooled 95% interval above zero versus stock and R02. These are proposed study thresholds. Report $L_{99}-L_1$ and all four signed $L_p-L_{50}$ errors as secondary outcomes. Prediction: on strong isolated ridges, the stock span tends to exceed the measured span, and the candidate reduces that error. Multi-ridge and clear paths have $w=0$.
- Positive controls are a resolved triangular crest, a degenerate leg with zero spread, and independent symmetric normal test distributions with known summed variance. Zero controls are smooth-Earth blockage, two competing crests, and identical input reversed end to end. Sweep ridge height, a neighboring crest, spacing on a synthetic analytic ridge, and all scored quantiles. The stock battery exercises 50% time. Require continuity, monotonicity and reciprocity for the tails too. For implementation verification, TN 101 §7.5 tabulates a convolved full-path 10/50/90% loss of 184.8/191.3/195.5 dB. The table tests the historical construction with its published leg distributions.
- Compare independence with a diagnostic of perfectly shared weather, pairing the two leg deviates at identical rank. This bounds one dependence scenario. Require predicted zero median changes on all links and unchanged censored outcomes. A general spread reduction that R02 matches, transfer to one receiver group alone, or a gain confined to 1971/profile-disagreement rows defeats the broad claim. If strong-ridge support is too sparse to assess dispersion across independent receiver groups, report a limited mechanism test. Promotion still requires the repository's full and screened paired gains and all regular gates. A confirmed shape effect with negligible composite change can remain a scientific finding.
