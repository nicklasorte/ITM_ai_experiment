# H01 · Coupled diffraction by two resolved ridges

**Question.** Can the field transmitted past a second, separated ridge explain excess loss on mountain paths after accounting for the first ridge, frequency, and receiver sensitivity? A positive answer would justify a targeted correction and establish where a single equivalent edge loses physical information.

**Starting evidence.** Run 502 restored the actual-path ITM roughness term \(A_{fo}\). Its composite was 11.13 dB and its censored violation rate was 47.2%. On R2 its measured-path mean error was −6.0 dB. D03 found that several edges predicted a higher fraction of non-detections within bands of similar Bullington loss. The number of edges also covaries with path length and frequency. Run 501 summed edge losses and produced long-path changes of hundreds of decibels. Runs 202 and 505 used local single-edge approximations. The identifying change here is a joint complex field for two real crests. The published theory applies to thin absorbing screens, small diffraction angles and sufficiently separated obstacles. Terrain, scatter and foliage can violate those assumptions. ## Freeze geometry before opening outcomes

Read the link's frozen profile by \(\texttt{path_id}\), use the indexed sample spacing, and interpolate the bare terrain \(g(x)\) linearly. \(D\) is the profile endpoint distance, all lengths are metres. Insert \(D/2\) by interpolation. On each half subtract the line through that half's two ground endpoints and retain the positive relief \(q_j(x)\). The **diagnostic** cohort has one connected interval of positive relief on each half, each with one interior local maximum, counting a level plateau once at its midpoint. Put a screen at each maximum. Exclude ties between disconnected maxima. Require both tops to obstruct the terminal ray and the valley to clear the crest-to-crest chord by at least 0.6 of the first-zone radius at the middle of that subpath. This last condition is a proposed valley-depth scale. Report row and path counts by site, band and censor status only after fixing membership. Archive excluded geometries as controls with a reason. D03's “two edges” class uses a different definition. [ITU-R P.526-16, §§2.1 and 2.5](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I/en)

## Compute the coupled field

Use \(g_e(x)=g(x)+x(D-x)/(2a_e)\), with the effective radius from the frozen ITM call. At each crest subtract the straight line joining the two absolute antenna heights from \(g_e\) to get signed \(h_1,h_2\). The distances between transmitter, crests and receiver are \(a,b,c>0\), with \(a+b+c=D\). Use \(\lambda=299.792458/f_{\rm MHz}\). For a positive segment length \(s\), define \(K_s(y)=\exp(i\pi y^2/(\lambda s))/\sqrt{i\lambda s}\) with the principal square root. The normalized field for the two apertures is

\[
F_{12}=\frac{1}{K_D(0)}\int_{h_2}^{\infty}K_c(-z)
             \int_{h_1}^{\infty}K_b(z-y)K_a(y)\,dy\,dz .
\]

This is the two-screen specialization of Vogler's paraxial field, expressed with the conjugate time convention. Use the improved evaluation in TR‑83‑124 §3, or an independently verified contour quadrature. Direct finite truncation of the oscillatory tails can manufacture attenuation. For a single screen at \(x\), use \(v=h\sqrt{2D/[\lambda x(D-x)]}\) and \(F_1(v)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(e^{-i\pi/4}\sqrt{\pi/2}\,v)\). Define the signed change \(\delta=-20\log_{10}|F_{12}|+20\log_{10}|F_1(\max(v_1,v_2))|\). Its sign can vary with geometry. This incremental construction is proposed here. It preserves 502's empirical Bullington correction and \(A_{fo}\) without adding the full obstruction loss twice. [Vogler, eqs. 5–8, 14–19 and 28](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/83-124_ocr.pdf) · [DeMinco et al., §§2–3](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/TR-26-580.pdf)

## Define a full-domain candidate before scoring

A binary eligibility switch would recreate B05's discontinuities. For the proposed full-domain predictor estimate each half's screen location from \(\bar x_j=\int xq_j^2 dx/\int q_j^2 dx\), integrating the piecewise-linear relief with its zeros inserted. Use the actual interpolated ground at that coordinate. This symmetric estimator deliberately sacrifices coverage of paths with both dominant ridges in one half. It can place a screen in a valley when a half has several crests. Verify the effect against the true-crest diagnostic before proceeding.

Write \(S(u)=3t^2-2t^3,\ t=\min(1,\max(0,u))\). Let \(P_j=\max q_j/\sqrt{\lambda D/4}\) and \(V\) be the largest positive depth of \(g_e(x)\) below the chord between the effective screens divided by \(\sqrt{\lambda b/4}\). Include interior profile vertices and screen endpoints when taking the maximum. Define \(W=S(P_1)S(P_2)S(V/0.6)S(\min(v_1,v_2))\). In the future proposed variant, add \(W\delta\) to 502's reference attenuation before its unchanged variability operation. If a half has zero relief area or the two locations coincide, the limiting correction is zero and the predictor returns 502. The smooth comparison profile has zero positive relief. Continuous weights, the half-path partition and squared-relief centroids are fixed design choices with zero fitted parameters. Check their limits. Fail on numerical nonconvergence or nonfinite loss. Area mode has no terrain profile and is outside the candidate's claim.

## Test and decide

1. **Verify the field independently.** Removing both screens must yield unit field, one grazing screen 6.0206 dB. Two equispaced coplanar grazing screens give 9.5424 dB because \(|F|=1/3\). The independent grazing sum gives 12.0412 dB. Reverse each path, lower either screen, bring two identical screens together and vary heights continuously through grazing. Require agreement with a second numerical implementation within a proposed 0.05 dB on a preregistered synthetic grid, including asymmetric separations and deep shadow. Numerical failure stops the candidate. [Vogler, eqs. 59–61 and Table 1](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/81-86_ocr.pdf)
2. **Verify the interface.** Future hook points are the delta constructor in \(\texttt{itm_variant/itm_p2p_core_exp.m}\), an opt-in switch in \(\texttt{params_exp.m}\), and a profile-only helper. Sweep double peaks through the midpoint, add a flat shoulder, raise a shallow third peak, and reverse the terrain. Compare oracle-crest and centroid increments on the diagnostic cohort. A reversal of the predicted median correction there or a failed frozen physics check stops field scoring. Record \(a,b,c,h_1,h_2,v_1,v_2,\delta,W\), support counts and computation time.
3. **Test the mechanism.** Prespecify that measured R2 rows underpredicted by 502 on average should move toward zero as secondary obstruction increases. For R1, R3 and VA predict smaller changes on low-relief controls. Tabulate signed errors across the entire eligible population, with fixed site and frequency bands, and compare repeated frequencies on the same path. Bootstrap whole paths. A benefit confined to one path, opposite changes in censored bounds, or loss of the relationship within distance/frequency strata defeats the proposed explanation. Report signed non-detection margin \(L_{50}-L_{\rm bound}\) independently of measured-row RMS.
4. **Separate mechanism evidence from a keep.** Compare to 502 to isolate coupling, to 000 for the repository's keep decision, and to R02 as comparator. The 502 parent violates 966 of 2,045 bounds. The gate permits at most 593 given stock's 573, requiring a net 373 repairs. If fewer than 373 parent violations have \(W>0\), a global keep is arithmetically impossible. A local mechanism result can still be reported. Preserve all frozen rows, both composites, site limits, physics tests and path-paired uncertainty.
