# H05 · Finite curvature of an isolated ridge

**Decision.** Does the breadth of one mountain crest explain a repeatable frequency-dependent loss after holding its diffraction angle and terminal geometry fixed? This proposed matched-path experiment has zero fitted propagation parameters. Its output is a decision about whether a full ITM variant merits development.

## What the experiment can distinguish

B05 proposed a rounded-crest term, then deferred it when two equation transcriptions disagreed. Run 501 accumulated knife-edge losses over successive obstructions. Run 502 retained Bullington’s empirical correction and restored ITM’s terrain-dependent $A_{fo}$ term. It still violated 68.3% of the 41 R2 censored rows in the 0–1-edge bin. That bin mixes geometries and contains repeated paths. It motivates an isolation test and supplies no independent estimate of the curvature effect. Dougherty and Maloney compared rounded crests with VHF measurements on two Denver-area alignments. Their paper identifies radius estimation and rough crests as unresolved questions. ## Geometry and calculation

1. Use the frozen ordered elevations, actual profile spacing, structural terminal heights, polarization and ITM effective radius $a_e=1/\gamma_e$. Use $\lambda=299.792458/f_{\rm MHz}$ metres. Find the stretched string by P.526-16 Annex 1 equation (44). Group consecutive string samples into one obstruction under §4.4. Require exactly one interior group before any valley-merging heuristic. Save the two tangent contacts and, by Attachment 1 equations (107)–(116), the projected tangent intersection. The distances $d_1,d_2$ and positive height $h$ above the terminal chord refer to this *vertex*. The physical crest used to estimate radius is a separate point. Restrict the horizontal-distance approximation to both arms within 5° of horizontal and diffraction angle below 0.2 rad. Apply the effective-Earth correction once. [P.526-16, Annex 1 §§4.1–4.4, Figs. 8c and 14, Attachment 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I!!PDF-E.pdf)
2. Identify one locally convex top with a summit between the tangent contacts. On its effective-Earth profile, take horizontal offsets $x_i$ from the summit and positive vertical drops $y_i$. Follow each flank to its first drop of one local first Fresnel radius $F_1=\sqrt{\lambda d_1 d_2/(d_1+d_2)}$. A window of exactly $F_1$ is a proposed convention. Compute $R=N^{-1}\sum_i x_i^2/(2y_i)$ from the two flanks. Equation (38) displays an arithmetic mean although the surrounding prose calls it a median. Use the displayed equation. Plateaus, equal candidate summits, absent flanks and division by zero yield an unresolved radius. Retain a sensitivity estimate based on median ratios and another at half the depth. Record the radius separately for each frequency. [P.526-16, Annex 1 §§2.1, 4.2, equations (2), (37)–(38), Fig. 10](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I!!PDF-E.pdf)
3. Use metres throughout. Let $s=1/d_1+1/d_2$, $Q=\pi R/\lambda$, $m=Rs/Q^{1/3}$, $n=(h/R)Q^{2/3}$, $u=mn$ and $\nu=h\sqrt{2s/\lambda}$. These quantities except $s$ are dimensionless. For $\nu>0$, use P.526 equation (31) for $J(\nu)$. The control predicts $J$ dB excess loss and the treatment predicts $J+T$ at the same vertex. Set $B=7.2\sqrt m-2m+3.6m^{3/2}-0.8m^2$. Use $T=B+12.5u$ for $u\le4$, and $T=B+17u-6-20\log_{10}u$ for $u>4$. At zero radius set $T=0$ by its analytic limit. These signs and divisions were checked on the printed PDF page 21. Applying $T$ directly to 502 would combine it with a different empirical obstruction correction and obscure this contrast. [P.526-16, Annex 1 §4.2, equations (31)–(36)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I!!PDF-E.pdf)

## Evidence the terrain can support

Freeze eligibility from geometry before inspecting losses. For each arm examine clearance on terrain outside the modeled crest using Attachment 2’s trimming and the 0.6-first-Fresnel-radius criterion. Check penumbra overlap on both sides. A proposed conservative screen uses $w(K)=(\lambda K^2/\pi)^{1/3}$ from §2.2, with local radius $K=R$ on the crest and $K=a_e$ on a smooth terminal arm. Require each arm length to exceed the sum of these two widths. Flag departures from the local smooth-surface assumption. For each terrain segment on either arm, mirror an endpoint in its line and test whether the stationary reflection point lies on the segment and both ray legs clear intervening terrain. Flag such profiles as reflection-contaminated. Compare surface-fit residuals with the §2.4 smoothness limit $0.04(R\lambda^2)^{1/3}$ m. Require at least two independent surveyed or DEM samples on each flank inside the window. The fixed Phase 2 grid interpolates surveys to 30 m. Count original survey vertices from the source coordinates for this support audit. The prediction still uses the frozen profile. Mark unknown native spacing, terrain precision or transverse reflection conditions. A passing profile screen establishes conditional one-dimensional evidence. A 30 m profile cannot verify subpixel smoothness, crest width across the path, or absence of out-of-plane reflections. Report admitted, excluded and unresolved counts by site, distinct path and frequency. ## Preregistered contrast and falsification

Within each Phase 2 path match transmitter and receiver heights and polarization, and require the same physical crest at both frequencies. Select the lowest and highest geometry-eligible frequencies before viewing censor status. For detected pairs subtract free-space loss from each measured basic loss, then take the high-minus-low difference $D$. Calculate $r=D-(J_H-J_L)$ and $\Delta T=T_H-T_L$. The prediction is that $|r|-|r-\Delta T|$ is positive across paths. Give each path equal weight after averaging its configurations. Report site-specific effects and a paired path-bootstrap interval. Both signs of $\Delta T$ are possible. Only compare the predicted sign where the primary and radius-sensitivity estimates agree. Flag changes in sign, calibration or recording conditions across frequencies, and leave-one-site-out reversals. An upper-frequency nondetection with a detected lower frequency gives a lower bound on $D$. Reversing which frequency was detected gives an upper bound. Two lower bounds give no bound on the difference. Keep these counts and one-sided incompatibilities separate from detected-pair errors.

Controls include an analytic parabola, the $R\to0$ limit, equal geometry under terminal reversal, and the 0.0412 dB mismatch of the two polynomial limits at $u=4$. Flat terrain and multiple separated crests should produce no eligible single-crest observations. Report whether independent sites contain matched paths. Use the frozen sites for development diagnostics and repeat the preregistered contrast in an independent campaign for transfer. A nonpositive gain there, sign reversal under plausible radius estimates, or inability to resolve the crest prevents a transfer claim. Insufficient exposure leaves the hypothesis open.

## Handoff decision

A future MATLAB worker could reuse `multi_edge_diffraction_rev1` geometry and its `knife_J` formula, and expose the geometric diagnostics beside the two excess-loss estimates. Keep this proposal at mechanism-test status until an all-path transition has been specified and passes the existing continuity, reciprocity and censor checks. A selector that returns stock on hard-to-model rows can introduce jumps at the eligibility boundary. For any proposed integration confined to these paths, calculate its maximum possible censor improvement before implementation. Starting at 502’s reported 47.2%, reaching the repository’s current 29.0% ceiling requires recovering roughly 38.6% of its existing violations even with zero new ones. Use exact stored counts for the feasibility decision. A successful matched contrast can survive as physical evidence if its reach rules out a full-model gain.
