# Batch B01: diffraction at the path's own geometry

Planner: Claude Fable 5.2, 2026-11-21. Worker: Haiku. Scope: hypotheses H1,
H2, and H3 in [docs/WORKING_PAPER_FINDINGS.md](../../docs/WORKING_PAPER_FINDINGS.md).
The predictions below were written and committed before any scored run of
these switches.

## Why this batch

Stock ITM overpredicts loss on diffraction paths: site R2 has a mean error
of +10.6 dB (RMS 28.6), and experiment D01 found long-term paths where ITM
is 70–90 dB high while P.2001 is within about 20 dB. R02 showed that
retuning constants inside the gates buys 0.1 dB, so the fix has to be
structural.

ITM's diffraction loss is `w·A_se + (1 − w)·A_k + A_fo`. It is never
evaluated at the path distance d. ITM evaluates it at two longer distances,
d_3 and d_4 (at least 5·(a_e²/f)^(1/3) beyond the horizons, about 21 km at
1 GHz), and extrapolates a straight line back to d.

Two planner findings shape the batch:

1. **H2 as first written can't be run.** Evaluating ITM's own formulas at
   the path distance is degenerate for a single-obstacle path: there
   d = d_L1 + d_L2, so ITM's knife-edge parameter is zero for any obstacle
   height (A_k = 12.04 dB), and the smooth-earth term divides zero by zero.
   ITM's double knife edge only has meaning beyond the horizons, which is
   why ITM extrapolates. So "diffraction at the path distance" needs a
   knife-edge loss computed from the profile. H1 and H2 are therefore tested
   together: experiment 101 uses ITM's own two obstacles (the nearest test
   of H2), and 102–104 change which edges are used (H1).
1. **The extrapolation biases high.** Knife-edge loss is concave in
   distance beyond the horizons, so a line through two far points lies
   above the curve when extended back toward the obstacle. ITM also gives
   each of its two edges the whole bending angle. Both push predicted loss
   up, which matches the sign of the R2 error.

## What the new switches do

Code: `itm_variant/multi_edge_diffraction_rev1.m` (tests in
`itm_variant/tests/test_diffraction_rev1.m`, 9 of 9 pass) and the
`cfg.ctx` blocks in `itm_variant/itm_p2p_core_exp.m`. With every switch at
`"itm"` the variant is unchanged (parity 6.8 × 10⁻¹³ dB after the edits).

- `cfg.diffraction ≠ "itm"`, on transhorizon paths only: the smooth-earth
  and clutter part `w·A_se + A_fo` keeps ITM's line through d_3 and d_4.
  The knife-edge part becomes `(1 − w(d))·A_profile`, where `A_profile` is
  the profile-based loss at the path's own geometry and w(d) is ITM's
  weight at d. The diffraction line keeps its slope M_d and is re-anchored
  through the new value at d, so the line-of-sight fit and the scatter
  crossover work as before. On smooth terrain w = 1 and nothing changes.
- `cfg.line_of_sight = "profile_horizons"`: a path whose profile blocks the
  direct ray is treated as transhorizon, even where the smooth-earth
  horizons of ITM's effective heights say line of sight.
- No new free parameters. Every constant is from the cited equation.

| Switch value | Source |
| --- | --- |
| `"epstein_peterson"` | Epstein and Peterson, Proc. IRE 41(5), 1953; NTIA TR-26-580 (2025) section 3.3.2. Edges: the two radio-horizon obstacles. ν and J: ITU-R P.526-16 section 4.1, eq. (26) and (31) |
| `"deygout"` | Deygout, IEEE Trans. Antennas Propag. 14(4), 1966; NTIA TR-26-580 section 3.3.3. Principal edge plus one on each side, selected as in ITU-R P.526-11 section 4.4.2, eq. (49), (49a) |
| `"p526_cascaded"` | ITU-R P.526-11 section 4.4.2, eq. (50a), (50b), (51), (52) |
| `"bullington"` | ITU-R P.526-16 section 4.5.1, eq. (49)–(57) |
| `"profile_horizons"` | Horizon test of ITM's FindHorizons (NTIA ITM v1.4) applied to the region decision |

## Worker procedure, same for every experiment

1. Parent for 101–105 is `best`, which is `000-baseline` (stock ITM; R02
   wasn't kept). Create branch `exp/NNN-short-name` in the cloud clone.
1. Add the LOG entry first: ID, tier 2, function, the rationale sentence and
   predictions copied from this file.
1. Edit only the one line (two lines for 106) of `itm_variant/params_exp.m`
   that the experiment names. Don't touch any other file.
1. Copy `itm_variant/params_exp.m` to the Mac (write to a new folder under
   `/mnt/user-data/outputs/`, wait 5 s, `device_commit_files` with
   `force`), and confirm the MD5 on the Mac matches before running.
1. Run on the Mac as a batch job:
   `run_eval_rev1(Tag="NNN-short-name", Best="000-baseline")`. For 106, pass
   the tag chosen by its parent rule as `Best` only if that run was kept;
   otherwise `Best="000-baseline"`.
1. Read `experiments/NNN-short-name/summary.txt`. Apply the acceptance rule
   in FRAMEWORK.md exactly: physics battery PASS; composite and screened
   composite each improve by at least 0.3 dB over best; both paired
   intervals above zero; no site RMS worse by more than 0.5 dB; censored
   violations at most 1.0 point above best; rationale logged first.
1. Bring the experiment folder and `results/archive_rev1.*` back (tar on the
   Mac, stage, unpack), finish the LOG entry with the outcome and whether
   each prediction held, append the `results.tsv` row (20 fields; record the
   R02 comparison in the decision field), run the guard script, and commit
   with `Agent-Role: worker`. Keep: merge to `best`. Reject: discard, or
   rename `arc/NNN-short-name` if the archive lists it on the front or as a
   cell elite.
1. Restore `cfg.diffraction = "itm"` and `cfg.line_of_sight = "itm"` on the
   Mac before the next experiment unless that experiment's parent is a kept
   run.

**Stop and hand back to the planner if:** a MATLAB error or a job that fails
twice; the summary lacks any line the rule needs; parity of stock ITM is in
doubt; a score moves by more than 5 dB at R1, R3, or VA (not expected by any
prediction); the 106 parent rule doesn't pick exactly one run; or anything
here is unclear. Infrastructure failures are retried once and logged as
`infra`.

## Experiments

Predictions use stock ITM as the reference: composite 16.0, screened 13.4,
Phase 2 cell 18.3, long-term level 24.6, shape 5.2, censored 28.0 %; site
RMS R1 13.3, R2 28.6 (mean +10.6), R3 9.0, VA 15.4.

### 101-ep-two-horizon
- Change: `cfg.diffraction = "epstein_peterson";`
- Tier 2. Function: `longley_rice` diffraction line; `multi_edge_diffraction_rev1`.
- Source: Epstein and Peterson 1953; NTIA TR-26-580 section 3.3.2.
- Rationale: the same two horizon obstacles ITM uses, but with each edge's
  own geometry at the path distance instead of a line extrapolated from
  20 km or more beyond the horizons.
- Predicted: R2 mean error falls from +10.6 to between +3 and +8 dB, R2 RMS
  to 24–27. R1, R3, and VA RMS move by less than 0.5 dB. Phase 2 cell
  16.5–17.8. Long-term level 21–24, with the D01 Sutton Coldfield rows
  (LT2116) at least 20 dB lower. Shape within ±0.1. Composite 14.5–15.6.
  Censored violations rise to 30–40 %; this is the most likely reason for
  rejection. Continuity: possible step where a sweep crosses from the
  line-of-sight region to transhorizon.
- Confidence: medium on direction, low on size.

### 102-deygout-three-edge
- Change: `cfg.diffraction = "deygout";`
- Tier 2. Same functions. Alternative to 101 and 103.
- Source: Deygout 1966; NTIA TR-26-580 section 3.3.3; edge selection per
  ITU-R P.526-11 section 4.4.2.
- Rationale: the principal obstruction and one edge on each side capture
  multi-ridge mountain paths that two horizon edges miss or double count.
- Predicted: loss at or above 101 on multi-ridge paths, so R2 mean error
  between +4 and +9 dB and R2 RMS 24–27; other sites within 0.5 dB.
  Long-term level 21–24. Censored 29–38 %. Composite 14.6–15.7.
- Confidence: medium on direction, low on the ordering against 101.

### 103-p526-cascaded
- Change: `cfg.diffraction = "p526_cascaded";`
- Tier 2. Same functions. Alternative to 101 and 102.
- Source: ITU-R P.526-11 section 4.4.2, eq. (50a)–(52).
- Rationale: Deygout's three edges with the ITU-R empirical correction,
  which adds up to 10 + 0.04·D dB when the principal edge is strong.
- Predicted: the highest loss of 101–103, so the smallest gain and the
  smallest censored increase: R2 mean error +7 to +11 dB, R2 RMS 26–29,
  censored 28–33 %, composite 15.2–16.1. The one most likely to pass the
  censored gate and the least likely to clear 0.3 dB.
- Confidence: medium.

### 104-bullington
- Change: `cfg.diffraction = "bullington";`
- Tier 2. Same functions. Diagnostic bound; expected to be rejected.
- Source: ITU-R P.526-16 section 4.5.1, eq. (49)–(57).
- Rationale: a single equivalent edge is the low-loss end of the knife-edge
  family and the core of the P.452 and P.2001 diffraction models, so it
  shows how much of their Phase 2 advantage this substitution explains.
- Predicted: R2 mean error between −2 and +5 dB; Phase 2 cell 15–17;
  censored above 40 %, failing the gate. If censored stays under 33 %, tell
  the planner: the references' optimism then comes from elsewhere.
- Confidence: medium.

### 105-profile-horizon-region
- Change: `cfg.line_of_sight = "profile_horizons";` (diffraction stays `"itm"`)
- Tier 2. Function: `longley_rice` region test.
- Source: ITM v1.4 FindHorizons applied to the region decision; H3.
- Rationale: a path whose terrain blocks the direct ray isn't a two-ray
  path, whatever the smooth-earth horizons of the effective heights say.
- Predicted: affects only blocked paths that ITM places in its
  line-of-sight region. Composite within ±0.4 of stock; R2 and R3 move by
  less than 1.5 dB RMS in either direction; long-term nearly unchanged.
  Real chance of a continuity failure, because a path changes formula the
  moment terrain touches the ray. Not expected to be kept alone; it is here
  to price the region change before combining it.
- Confidence: low.

### 106-best-diffraction-with-profile-region
- Parent rule (pick exactly one of 101, 102, 103): the run with the lowest
  composite among those with physics PASS and censored ≤ 29.0 %. If none
  meets both, the lowest composite among those with physics PASS. If none
  passes physics, stop and hand back.
- Change: that run's `cfg.diffraction` value, plus
  `cfg.line_of_sight = "profile_horizons";`
- Rationale: with a profile-based knife edge available, blocked paths that
  ITM calls line of sight get an obstacle loss from their own geometry.
- Predicted: R2 and R3 improve by a further 0.3–1.5 dB RMS over the parent
  run; censored within 2 points of the parent run; continuity is the main
  risk.
- Confidence: low.
