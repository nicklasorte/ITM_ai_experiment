### Assumptions checked against the code (2026-11-27)

- A secondary edge's loss is the largest w·J(ν) among the candidates on
  its side of the principal edge (`isolated_secondary`,
  `multi_edge_diffraction_rev1.m`, from line 486):
  - ν is the Deygout parameter on the sub-path from the path end to the
    principal edge.
  - w = min(1, c / (0.6 r_1)), where c is the candidate's height above the
    deepest point between it and the principal edge, and r_1 is the first
    Fresnel radius there (ITU-R P.526-16 section 2.5).
  - In the full cascade, L_ba = J_p + T (L_t + L_r + C), with T = 1 −
    exp(−J_p / 6).
- `edges_rev1.csv` (B38) holds, for each row, the C2 arm's L_ba edges as
  [t p r] with their ν and w, or [p] alone. So T, L_t and L_r can be
  rebuilt from it. The rebuild must reproduce B38's Lba_U − Lba_P = T (L_t
  + L_r) to 1e-9 dB on every row, or the analysis stops.
- EVH codes 101 to 199 are tree heights of code − 100 m (LF2016 Remap EVH
  data dictionary, as `d30_build_evh_rev1.m` decodes them). Other codes
  give no tree height and count as 0 m of canopy.

## 2. D39a: separation of the secondary edges (model only)

**Population:** B36's R1 rows (1,427 on 29 paths) decide; VA's 243 rows
are descriptive.

**Per row:** from `edges_rev1.csv` and the profile, compute T, and w·J(ν)
for each secondary edge. Each secondary edge's contribution to L_ba is
T·w·J(ν). Its separation is the number of profile samples between it and
the principal edge.

**Statistic:** the share of R1's total secondary contribution (summed over
rows) that comes from secondary edges within 3 samples (about 90 m) of the
principal edge, as a ratio of sums, with a 95 % interval over paths (2,000
draws, seed 20261139, paths sorted, draws of shape (2000, 29)). The
3-sample cut is a planner choice; shares for 1, 2, 5 and 10 samples are
reported as descriptive results.

**Reading rule:** "adjacent" if the interval's lower end is above 0.5;
"separated" if its upper end is below 0.5; "mixed" otherwise.

## 3. D39b: canopy in the first Fresnel zone

**Population:** every Phase 2 row with siting O, not censored, with a 707
prediction (12,570 rows, as in D38b). No row is left out: q (below) is
defined on every row.

**Per row:** the ray, earth bulge and r_1 are as in D38b. Interior sample
i is canopy-only intruding if the bare-terrain clearance is at least r_1
but the canopy-top clearance (terrain plus EVH tree height) is less than
r_1. q is the share of interior samples that are canopy-only intruding.

**Statistic and rule:** as D38b section 3, with q in place of f_F and seed
20261139. Within each site, y = e_707 and x = q are demeaned within
frequency × obstructed strata (obstructed by bare terrain, as in D38b).
The slope b has a 95 % interval over paths. It's resolved if the interval
excludes zero and |b| ≥ 5 dB per unit share. Outcome **C−** if b is
resolved negative at two or more sites and positive at none; **C+** the
reverse; **none** otherwise. Also reported for stock, descriptive, and
with each site left out in turn (does the outcome depend on one site?).

### D39a: where the secondary edges sit (model only)

R1 (1,427 rows on 29 paths; the secondary contributions to L_ba sum to
3,442 dB, a mean of 2.41 dB per row):

| Within … samples of the principal edge | Share of secondary loss | 95 % interval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.000 | 0.000 to 0.000 |
| 2 | 0.000 | 0.000 to 0.001 |
| 3 (deciding) | 0.001 | 0.000 to 0.005 |
| 5 | 0.002 | 0.000 to 0.005 |
| 10 | 0.004 | 0.000 to 0.012 |

**Reading: separated.** On R1, almost none of the secondary-edge loss
comes from points within 300 m of the principal edge. That holds on the
diffraction-mode rows (0.000 within 3 samples) and the line-of-sight rows
(0.003). VA (descriptive) is the same: 0.000 within 3 samples and 0.046
within 10.

**This is partly by construction** (fresh reviewer). B06's isolation
weight gives w = 0 to the sample next to the principal edge and to every
point on its monotone flank. So "the same ridge counted twice" was ruled
out by design before D39a ran. On R1:

- On 87 % of the secondary loss, no point within 3 samples could have had
  w > 0. On 75 %, none within 10 samples could.
- Where a nearby point could have counted (13 % of the loss), the method
  still picked farther points: 1.1 % of the loss is within 3 samples of
  the first rising point past the valley.
- 39 % of the loss is within 100 samples (about 3 km), and 25 % comes
  from edges with w < 1 (shallow valleys that may belong to the same
  massif).

So the excess comes from secondary high points that are mostly separate
from the principal edge's flank (0.4 % of the loss within 10 samples on
R1, 4.6 % at VA), which B06's isolation test counts. Whether they should
add their loss in full is the open physics question (planner's reading;
untested). 707's cascade is P.526-11's eq. (50) form with B06's isolation
weights: each secondary is chosen by the largest w·J, not the largest ν,
and w is a linear ramp, a design choice (B06). No coding defect was found,
and the read of the equations against the P.526-11 PDF that B01 noted as
owed is still owed.

### What this means for improving ITM (planner's reading, not part of a rule)

- **Secondary-edge repair (R1).** A physical defect would have been the
  case for a repair, and there isn't one to point to. The secondary edges
  are mostly separate obstacles, which the cascade counts by design.
  A repair would trade P.526-11's construction for a fit to R1. Under the
  stop, and with the 1.13 dB headroom, it isn't worth a scored candidate.
- **Vegetation in the Fresnel zone.** The evidence is now one site (R2).
  R2 holds 707's largest errors (RMS 18.4 dB on B38's D38b rows), so a
  Fresnel-weighted canopy term is still the most promising physics lead
  for R2. A single-site association isn't enough to reopen the search.
  The next step that could be is a model-only build of such a term from a
  published vegetation model, such as ITU-R P.833's terrestrial-path
  forms, screened before any score. That needs Nick to lift the stop for
  it.
