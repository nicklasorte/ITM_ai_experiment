## Purpose

Phase 2 rows whose terrain is clear but whose terminals sit near woodland
are predicted with too little loss (headroom map M11; phase plan lead 2).
ITU-R P.833-10 gives the excess loss of a ray that runs through woodland.
The land cover along each profile became available with PR #10.

- `itm_variant/path_vegetation_rev1.m`: on each terminal's ray to its radio
  horizon (or each half of the direct ray on a line-of-sight path), the
  length d of samples that are woodland (NLCD 41, 42, 43, 90; B11's
  "trees_forest" mapping) and where the ray runs less than R = 15 m above
  the ground (ITU-R P.2108-1 Table 3, trees/forest), Earth bulge in the
  ray's chord frame. Loss per ray: P.833-10 §2.1 eq. (1),
  A_ev = A_m [1 - exp(-d gamma / A_m)], with gamma and A_m from P.833-10
  Table 1 (mixed forest, 105.9-2117.5 MHz; read from the rendered PDF
  2026-11-24), interpolated in log f and held outside the table. Summed over
  the two rays; an end that already has the P.2108 terminal clutter term
  (concealed mobile terminals) isn't counted.
- `itm_variant/landcover_profile_rev1.m`: per-sample classes from the
  run-length file, hash-checked, length-checked.
- Core: added to the reference attenuation after the bound and the
  terminal clutter term; `info.A_veg_db`. Long-term rows get 0 (no land

- Test `test_path_vegetation`: Table 1 values, the line-of-sight split, the

## Why it fails, and what would change that

- **Wrong geometry for open terminals.** P.833-10 §2.1 eq. (1) is for a
  terminal inside woodland, and A_m is the loss when the ray stays in the
  canopy (P.833-10 says A_m is equivalent to terminal clutter loss). Phase 2's
  open terminals were sited in the open, and the 30 m NLCD can't see a
  clearing of a few pixels. So the term gives terminal-clutter-sized losses
  (median 20-22 dB at R2 and VA open) on rows that need about 3 dB on
  average (VA) or nothing (R2).
- **The section that fits open terminals** is P.833-10 §3.1 eq. (7)
  (A_et = d gamma, at or below 1 GHz) capped by the loss of the lowest-loss
  path around the canopy, treated as a thin finite-width diffraction screen
  (P.526). Above 1 GHz, §3.2 uses radiative energy transfer with species
  parameters (Tables 5-8). Both need the canopy's height and extent near
  the terminal, which NLCD classes don't give.
- **Input that would make it testable:** a public canopy-height layer (for
  example LANDFIRE existing vegetation height, USGS) sampled along the
  profiles, so clearings and tree heights come from data instead of a
  class-wide R. That would be a `data/` PR for Nick. Until then, the family
  is closed on the current inputs.
