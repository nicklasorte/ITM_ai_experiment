# Batch B33: season pairing, quantile widths and transmitter-height sensitivity on OT 75-74 (D33, diagnostic, no score)

Planner: Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-11-26. Takes Package F and X03 from
[post_b32_ten_experiments_rev1.md](../proposals/post_b32_ten_experiments_rev1.md)
(X01, X06, X03). Nick: "Do what you can do" (2026-11-26).

**Status: registered 2026-11-26; run and closed 2026-11-26 (descriptive).**

D-series: nothing here is scored or counts toward k. The OT 75-74 data was
inspected in B31 and B32, so it isn't a fresh holdout; every result is
descriptive of these paths.

## 1. Questions

- **X01:** on the same paths, does measured 50 % loss change between the two
  seasons at Graham Mountain (Table 8, Sep-Oct 1973; Table 9, Apr 1974) and
  Hunter Liggett (Table 10, Jan-Feb 1974; Table 11, May-Jun 1974), and does
  the change differ between the 3 m and 10 m receivers?
- **X06:** is the seasonal shift in the median comparable to the
  within-season spread of the 10 % to 90 % values?
- **X03:** how much do 707's and stock ITM's C_hi = L(3 m) − L(10 m) change
  when the transmitter moves from 0.5 m to 0.7 m and 1.0 m?

## 2. Data and inputs

- Measurements: `experiments/D31-ot7574/ot7574_tables_rev1.csv`. A cell is
  used only if it isn't missing, a lower bound, questionable or uncertain.
  Graham R5 appears only in Table 8 and is excluded from X01 and X06.
- Same configuration across seasons: Tables 8 and 9, and 10 and 11, list
  identical distances on all 40 shared paths (B31), so terrain and geometry
  inputs are the same and both models predict a seasonal change of exactly
  zero. The script checks this from `D32-ot7574-heights/predictions_rev1.csv`.
  The report's equipment and calibration between seasons aren't checked
  here, so a seasonal change may be instrumental as well as environmental.
- X03 uses the B32 pairs, profiles and inputs
  (`D32-ot7574-heights/paths_rev1.csv`, `profiles_rev1.csv`), with the
  receiver at 3 and 10 m and the transmitter at 0.5, 0.7 and 1.0 m. No run
  below 0.5 m.

## 3. Registered analysis

Resampling: 2,000 bootstrap draws over paths, one generator with seed
20261126 (the proposal's seed), strata in the order listed. 95 % percentile
intervals. Strata with fewer than 10 paths are descriptive.

**X01.** Per path, band and height h: S(h) = L_later(h) − L_earlier(h) at
50 % (later minus earlier season), on paths where both seasons' cells pass.
J = S(3 m) − S(10 m), on paths where all four cells pass. Strata: area
(Graham, Hunter Liggett) × band (VHF, UHF). S at 0.35 m is reported as
measured only. A stratum's S or J is "resolved" when its mean is at least
1 dB in size and its interval excludes zero (1 dB is the proposal's
planning resolution, not a score rule).

**X06.** Per path, season, band and height, with all three quantile cells
passing: W = L(90 %) − L(10 %) and K = L(90 %) + L(10 %) − 2 L(50 %). Per
stratum: mean W in each season, mean ΔW and ΔK (later minus earlier) with
intervals, and the ratio of mean |S| to the mean within-season W on the
same paths. No mixture is constructed from quantiles.

**X03.** Per B32 deciding row pair (C_hi cells both passing, R5 excluded)
and model: ΔC(t) = C_hi(t) − C_hi(0.5 m) for t = 0.7 and 1.0 m. Reported:
the share of paths with |ΔC| < 1 dB at both t, per model, overall and by
table and band; and the 707 − stock difference of ΔC. Stop rule from the
proposal: if at least 90 % of paths change by less than 1 dB for both
models, the transmitter clamp is a minor concern for C_hi within 0.5 to
1.0 m (this says nothing below 0.5 m).

## 4. Predictions (planner, before the run)

- X01: at least one stratum has a resolved S at 3 m or 10 m: 60 %. J
  resolved in any stratum: 30 %.
- X06: the ratio of mean |S| to mean W is below 0.5 in every stratum: 70 %.
- X03: the 90 % stop rule holds for both models: 75 %.

## 5. Checks before the run

1. Season inputs are identical: for every Table 8/9 and 10/11 path, the
   B32 predictions are equal across seasons for both models (differences
   below 1e-9 dB); stop if not.
2. X03 parity: the 0.5 m transmitter rerun reproduces B32's predictions
   within 1e-6 dB (profile serialization).
3. The analysis script's self-test recovers a planted seasonal shift and a
   planted zero shift on synthetic data.

## 6. Results

Scripts: `d33_tx_sensitivity_rev1.m` (Mac), `d33_analysis_rev1.py`. Outputs:
`experiments/D33-ot7574-seasons/` (`x01_season_shift_rev1.csv`,
`x01_interaction_rev1.csv`, `x06_quantile_width_rev1.csv`,
`x03_tx_sensitivity_rev1.csv`, `summary_rev1.json`) and
`experiments/D32-ot7574-heights/tx_sensitivity_rev1.csv`.

Checks before the run:

1. Season inputs: on all 480 season-paired model rows, both models give the
   same loss in both seasons (largest difference 0 dB).
2. X03 parity. The first try read the profiles back from
   `profiles_rev1.csv` and stopped: the 0.5 m rerun differed from B32 by up
   to 0.197 dB on 12 of 840 rows, in stock ITM as well as 707. A freshly
   sampled profile differs from the CSV copy by at most 5e-13 m but gives
   B32's loss exactly, so on a few paths ITM's loss jumps under rounding at
   the 1e-13 m level (a near-tie in its terrain analysis; the cause wasn't
   traced further). The script now resamples the profiles, and parity holds
   to 5e-13 dB. B32 used in-memory profiles, so its results don't change;
   B32's section 6b rerun used the CSV profiles and matched within 3e-7 dB
   on its 420 rows.
3. Self-test: PASS (a planted 3 dB shift is resolved; a zero shift isn't;
   no interaction is resolved in either case).

Interaction J = S(3 m) − S(10 m): Graham VHF **+2.15** (+0.07 to +4.20,
29 paths); Graham UHF **−2.75** (−4.86 to −0.68, 17 paths); Hunter Liggett

**X06, quantile widths** (paths where all three quantile cells pass in both
seasons; 12 to 27 paths per stratum): the ratio of mean |S| to the mean
within-season 10 % to 90 % width W is 0.30 to 1.05. At Graham UHF the width
is smaller in April than in Sep-Oct: ΔW −4.49 dB at 10 m (−6.61 to −2.20,
12 paths) and −2.51 dB at 3 m (−4.22 to −0.55, 14 paths). At Hunter
Liggett, ΔW is within ±0.16 dB at every height and band except UHF 0.35 m
(−1.15 dB, −2.11 to −0.35, 18 paths). Full table in
`x06_quantile_width_rev1.csv`.

**X03, transmitter height** (B32 deciding C_hi support, 325 path-band-table
cells): 92.9 % (707) and 92.3 % (stock ITM) change by less than 1 dB at both
0.7 and 1.0 m, so the registered stop rule holds overall. Median |ΔC| at
1.0 m: 0.01 dB (707) and 0.23 dB (stock). The exception is Eglin (Table 7):
78 to 82 % for 707 and 73 to 77 % for stock, with the largest change at
1.0 m 6.3 dB (stock, UHF). The largest 707 changes are at Hunter Liggett (4.8 to 5.4 dB).

What the results show, scoped to these paths:

- **Seasonal change exists in the measurements.** Four of the eight
  10 m and 3 m strata have a resolved seasonal shift of 1.4 to 2.2 dB in
  size. At Hunter Liggett, May-Jun has more loss than Jan-Feb at 10 m in
  both bands. Both models predict zero change, by construction. The
  equipment and calibration in each season weren't checked, so the shift
  may be instrumental as well as environmental.
- **The height response changes with season at Graham only, with opposite
  signs by band** (J +2.15 dB at VHF on 29 paths, −2.75 dB at UHF on 17;
  16 paths are in both). Opposite signs on largely overlapping paths don't
  fit one simple foliage effect; this is descriptive, on data already
  inspected in B31 and B32.
- **Season shifts are comparable to the time spread.** The 0.5 ratio
  predicted wasn't met: mean |S| is 0.30 to 1.05 times W. Mean |S| includes
  measurement noise, which pushes the ratio up.
- **The transmitter clamp is a minor concern for C_hi between 0.5 and
  1.0 m, except at Eglin**, where 18 to 27 % of paths change by 1 dB or more.
  Nothing here describes behavior below 0.5 m.
- 12 S and J strata (8 S, 4 J) and 12 X06 strata were tested without a multiplicity
  correction, so one or two resolved results could arise by chance.

Predictions against outcome:

- X01 any S resolved (60 %): yes. J resolved anywhere (30 %): yes (Graham).
- X06 ratio below 0.5 everywhere (70 %): no.
- X03 stop rule holds (75 %): yes overall; not at Eglin.

### 6c. Against Nick's adversarial review (a317a82), after the run

The review of the ten proposals landed after B33 was registered (3e70552)
and asks for several revisions to X01, X03 and X06. Two were checked on the
data after the run; they're sensitivity checks, not part of the rule.

- **Percentile orientation:** all 870 complete, unflagged 10/50/90 %
  triples are in increasing order (q10 ≤ q50 ≤ q90), so W and K have the
  signs section 3 assumes. The report's own definition of the percentages
  wasn't reread.
- **Hunter Liggett without T7** (the moved transmitter, excluded in the
  review's primary analysis; same seed, so the draws differ from section
  6): S at 10 m is +1.93 dB at VHF (+0.81 to +3.03, 32 paths, resolved) and
  +1.20 dB at UHF (−0.08 to +2.49, 30 paths, no longer resolved); S at 3 m
  at VHF is +1.46 dB (+0.14 to +2.91, 28 paths, now resolved). J stays
  unresolved in both bands. So Hunter Liggett's VHF shift holds without T7,
  but which strata clear the 1 dB rule depends on T7.
- **Calibration and J:** a calibration offset common to both heights in a
  season cancels in J; a height- or frequency-specific one doesn't. Graham's
  opposite-sign J by band could come from band-specific calibration; B33
  can't separate that from propagation.
- **Wording:** an S or J that isn't resolved is inconclusive, not evidence
  of no change; no equivalence claim is made.
- Not done from the review: the period and calibration metadata audit,
  censor bounds, terminal-level dependence, and a T7-inclusion sensitivity
  for X03.
