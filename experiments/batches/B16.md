## B16a: fixed-geometry coupled two-screen oracle (H01)

**Question.** Does coupled diffraction over the two ridges between which
707's principal edge switches explain the 30 dB step in 707's loss, when the
ridges are held fixed?

**Parent geometry.** `P2_R2_R2-020-T1_O` and `P2_R2_R2-020-T2_O`, the two
paths with 75 % of 707's real-path height-gain failures (LOG, 2026-11-22).
Transmitter height from the data at each frequency (6.6 m below 910 MHz, 7.3 m from 910 MHz; corrected before the run, the first launch stopped on it); receiver height swept 1 to 40 m
in 0.05 m steps; all seven R2 frequencies (230 to 9190 MHz). 30 m profiles
from `data/profiles`, ITM's effective Earth radius 1/gamma_e.

**Screens (frozen from geometry, before any measurement is read).** At
9190 MHz, the 707 principal edges just below and just above the largest
step of 707's L_ba on the sweep. A model-only look at 707 (planner, before
registration) shows these are profile points 650 and 624 on T1 (switch
between 8.85 and 8.90 m) and 668 and 289 on T2 (between 20.35 and 20.40 m);
the script selects them by the rule, not by these numbers. The same two
points are used at every height and frequency.

**Method.** Vogler's attenuation function for N = 2 (NTIA TR-83-124
§§2-3; Radio Science 17(6), 1982), new function
`itm_variant/vogler_two_screen_rev1.m`, with the Faddeeva function by
Weideman (SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 31(5), 1994) in
`itm_variant/faddeeva_w_rev1.m`. Earth curvature as the bulge
x (d - x) / (2 a_e) (P.526-11 eq. (49a)). Not wired into the model; no cfg
switch.

**Checks on the method (tests, before the sweep).** The five two-edge cases
21-25 of NTIA TR-26-580 Table 12 must reproduce its Table 17 Vogler values
within 0.0015 dB. N = 1 must equal the exact Fresnel knife edge (P.526-16
eq. (30)) within 1e-6 dB. Reversal must be exact. At large nu the loss must
tend to independent edges plus the spacing term (P.526-16 eq. (39)-(40)).
The Faddeeva function must match SciPy's `wofz` within 1e-12 relative.

**Estimands, at every sweep point.** Coupled loss, independent-edge loss
at the same coordinates (Epstein-Peterson with exact single-edge losses),
each screen alone, the spacing term, 707's L_ba and selected edges, 707's
and stock ITM's basic transmission loss at 50 %. Repeated after terminal
reversal.

**Stage 1 pass (numerical).** Integration tolerance 1e-10 against 1e-12
differs by less than 1e-3 dB, and the reversed oracle matches within
1e-6 dB, on every frequency. Continuity is reported as the largest change
between adjacent heights (0.05 m apart).

**Predictions (planner, before the run).** The coupled loss is continuous
on both paths (largest adjacent change well under 1 dB) where 707 steps by
about 30 dB at 9190 MHz. Coupled minus independent is a few dB, near the
spacing term. So coupled diffraction over the frozen pair doesn't produce
the step: the step is in 707's edge selection, not in the field.

**Stage 2 (measurement, only if stage 1 passes).** On frequencies with at
least two detected rows on each side of the switch height (in practice T1
below 9190 MHz; T2's 4595 and 9190 MHz rows are censored and its switch is
above the measured 1-13 m), the measured contrast mean(h >= switch) -
mean(h < switch) against each prediction's contrast at the measured
heights.

**Falsifier.** If the fixed-geometry oracle keeps a step of the same size
as 707's, or explains the measured contrast no better than 707, coupled
diffraction over this pair doesn't explain the logged jump. The review's
challenge stands: fixed screens make continuity nearly automatic, so a pass
here doesn't validate any terrain-to-screen selector. A repair candidate
needs its own predeclared selector with its own transition check.

**Script.** `experiments/batches/b16a_oracle_rev1.m`, output
`experiments/B16a-vogler-oracle/`.

## B16b: terrain irregularity estimator audit (H04)

**Question.** Does ITM's delta h change when the same piecewise-linear
profile is represented with different sample points?

**Design.** Every Phase 2 profile, with its rows' antenna heights. The
legacy estimator (ITM v1.4 QuickPfl and d1thx; NTIA TR-82-100) is copied
into the script and must reproduce the core's `info.delta_h_m` exactly on
every path, or the script stops. Tests, with the trim held fixed in metres
(estimator only) and end to end through the core (trim recomputed):
collinear refinement (each interval split in 2 and in 3), reversal, and
affine tilt (fixed trim only). The 2 x 2 of detrending {legacy, continuous
segment integrals} by quantiles {legacy sample, distance-weighted
generalized inverse with point masses for flat segments}, as the review
defines them. Short spans (fewer than two intervals, delta h = 0) are
counted separately. Analytic checks: a sinusoid over whole periods (true
interdecile range 2 sin(0.4 pi) A; a cosine, whose least-squares line is flat, so
the value is exact after detrending; changed from a sine after the first run,
because a sine over whole periods has a sloped least-squares line) and flat
terraces.

**Prediction (planner).** The legacy estimator resamples the profile's own
linear interpolant at a fixed number of points, so with the trim fixed it
should already be invariant to collinear refinement, reversal and tilt
within rounding. Any end-to-end change would come from the horizons that set
the trim, not from the estimator.

**Stop rule (review).** If the estimator is invariant within numerical
tolerance (0.01 m), stop: no model change. Feature differences of the 2 x 2
are reported before any score. No model change without held-out evidence.

**Script.** `experiments/batches/b16b_roughness_rev1.m`, output
`experiments/B16b-roughness-audit/`.

## B16c: rounded-crest eligibility count (H05)

**Question.** Are there enough independent paths and sites with one
resolved, isolated crest to run the rounded-crest diagnostic?

**Rule (frozen from geometry; no measured loss read).** On the native
survey vertices of `data/raw/its_phase2.zip`, for every (path, frequency,
heights) case in the link table:
1. one tangent-defined crest: both antennas' radio horizons are the same
   vertex, and it blocks the direct ray;
2. a native vertex on each flank within W = 250 m of the crest (the
   obstruction sample-spacing limit of P.526-16 §4.4); 500 m as a
   sensitivity;
3. both arms clear by 0.6 of the first Fresnel radius (P.526-16 §2.5,
   eq. (2));
4. documented vertical error small against the crest drops; a case without
   documented error is unresolved.

**Decision rule.** The diagnostic runs only if at least 5 distinct survey
profiles at 2 or more fixed sites pass rules 1-4 (independent path and
site replication, as the review asks; the numbers 5 and 2 are the planner's
design choice). Otherwise it's recorded as not supported by the data.

**Script.** `experiments/batches/b16c_eligibility_rev1.py`, output
`experiments/B16c-crest-eligibility/`.

## Results

### B16a (`experiments/B16a-vogler-oracle/`)

Method checks: 44 tests pass, including the five TR-26-580 two-edge cases
(to 0.001 dB) and a deep-clear second screen against SciPy. An independent
SciPy recomputation (`b16a_crosscheck_rev1.py`) of 1,094 sweep points agrees
to 1.9e-11 dB. Stage 1 passed on both paths (tolerance error under 2e-13 dB,
reversal exact).

| | T1 (screens 624 and 650, 780 m apart) | T2 (screens 289 and 668, 11.4 km apart) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 707 switch | 8.85 -> 8.90 m | 20.35 -> 20.40 m |
| 707 L_ba step at the switch | +14.7 dB (230 MHz) to +30.0 dB (9190 MHz) | +20.5 to +30.6 dB (910 to 9190 MHz) |
| Coupled oracle across the switch | -0.02 dB | -0.02 dB |
| Largest coupled change between heights 0.05 m apart | 0.05 to 0.29 dB | 0.05 to 0.31 dB |
| Coupled minus independent edges | 0.56 to 0.59 dB (spacing term 0.59 dB) | 0.01 to 0.02 dB |
| Coupled minus the worse screen alone | 17 to 33 dB | 19 to 35 dB |

- The coupled field over the frozen pair is continuous through 707's switch
  on both paths, at every frequency. Coupled minus independent is the
  spacing term, under 0.6 dB. So coupled diffraction doesn't make the step:
  it's 707's three-edge selection, which counts the third ridge (172 on T1,
  110 on T2) at full weight only after the principal edge moves.
- Second edges matter: the pair is 17 to 35 dB lossier than either screen
  alone, so a repair can't drop the secondary edges; it has to bring them
  in continuously.
- Stage 2, T1 (detected rows at 1-13 m, 8 below and 5 above the switch):
  measured contrast across the switch -5.1, -0.7, +2.8, +1.8, -3.3 and
  +0.1 dB (230 to 4595 MHz). 707 predicts +11.6 to +23.9 dB; the coupled
  pair -2.5 dB; stock ITM -2.4 to -5.2 dB. Mean absolute contrast error:
  707 17.7 dB, oracle 2.9 dB, stock 3.0 dB. The measurements contradict
  707's step at every frequency. T2 has no detected rows above its switch.
- Limits: the oracle level (72.7 dB on T1 at 9190 MHz) leaves out the third
  ridge and the rest of the terrain, so its level isn't a prediction. As the
  review says, fixed screens make continuity nearly automatic; this
  validates no selector.

**Next (repair candidate, to register separately):** a selector whose
secondary-edge contributions vary continuously with geometry, checked on
these two sweeps (largest adjacent change under 1 dB at 0.05 m) before any
score. Rivals: a continuous weighting of P.526-11's secondary edges, or
Vogler over a continuously weighted edge set.

### B16b (`experiments/B16b-roughness-audit/`)

- The script's copy of the estimator reproduces the core's delta h exactly
  on all 515 paths.
- **Not invariant to collinear refinement.** With the trim fixed, splitting
  every interval in 2 changes delta h by more than 0.01 m on 81 paths (in 3:
  90); largest 11.7 m (P2_R2_R2-005-T1_O, 3.0 %), median 0.7 %, 90th
  percentile 2.4 %, largest 5.1 % of delta h. Every changed path has a trim
  shorter than 242 intervals (7.3 km at 30 m). Cause: the number of
  resampled points, n = 10 p10 - 5 with p10 = fix(0.1 (span + 8)) capped at
  25, counts profile intervals, so on short trims the sample density, and
  with it the sample quantile, follows the input spacing. End to end
  (horizons recomputed) gives the same numbers: refinement doesn't move the
  horizons here.
- Reversal and affine tilt: invariant (largest 2e-10 m). No path uses the
  short-span rule (delta h = 0).
- The continuous estimator is invariant to refinement (2e-11 m) and
  recovers an analytic cosine's interdecile range to 0.01 m at 3 and 7.5 km
  periods (legacy: off by 0.2 to 1.0 m).
- 2 x 2 against legacy: median -0.3 %, 5th to 95th percentile -3.3 % to
  +2.1 % for the fully continuous form. Almost all of it is the quantile
  (LC is within 0.1 % of CC; CL is within 0.4 % of LL); the detrending
  barely matters.
- **Decision (stop rule):** the estimator isn't invariant, so the audit
  doesn't stop at step one, but in this repo the spacing is fixed at 30 m, so
  the dependence moves no score, and the continuous form changes delta h by
  a few percent. ITM's coefficients were calibrated with the legacy
  statistic. No model change without held-out evidence; recorded as a known
  representation dependence for anyone who runs ITM at another spacing.
- Consumers of delta h in the variant core, for the record: QuickPfl's
  line-of-sight horizon estimate, `diffraction_loss` (clutter term A_fo and
  the knife weight), `line_of_sight_loss`, `variability`, and the `_afo`
  delta forms. In 707 the delta reference uses a smooth path (delta h 0), so
  the actual path's delta h reaches 707 through `variability`, the ITM lines
  that set the troposcatter bound, and the line-of-sight horizons.

### B16c (`experiments/B16c-crest-eligibility/`)

- The 30 m profiles equal the survey vertices resampled linearly (largest
  difference 0.000 m), so the file mapping is right.
- Of 16,805 cases on 515 paths: line of sight 7,163; two horizons 6,138;
  flank vertex farther than 250 m 2,556; an arm not clear 798;
  geometry-eligible 150 cases on 12 survey profiles at 5 fixed sites (R2,
  R3, VA T1, T2, T4). No profile is eligible at all of its cases. With
  W = 500 m: 24 profiles at 8 sites.
- Rule 4: the survey headers give the map scale (1:24,000 for R1-R3,
  1:62,500 for VA) but no contour interval or vertical accuracy, so every
  case is unresolved. Sensitivity, if the maps met the 1947 National Map
  Accuracy Standards (90 % of elevations within half the contour interval)
  and "small" means both crest drops at least 3 times that error: 2 profiles
  at a 20 ft interval, 1 at 40 ft. Several eligible crests drop only 0 to
  6 m to a flank vertex, about one map error.
- **Decision:** not supported by the data (fewer than 5 profiles at 2
  sites under any reading). The rounded-crest diagnostic doesn't run. It
  would need surveyed crest profiles with documented vertical error.

## Stage 2: repair candidate design (2026-11-24; model only, nothing scored)

Nick asked for the repair candidate on 2026-11-24. Two parameter-free
selectors were tried on the B16a sweeps before registration. Neither passes
its own gate, so no candidate was registered or scored, and the Bonferroni
count k is unchanged.

**What a repair must do** (from B16a): keep the secondary edges (the pair is
17-35 dB lossier than either ridge alone) but stop the loss from jumping
when the highest-nu edge moves from one ridge to the other. Every
P.526-11-style three-edge cascade is continuous for a fixed principal edge
(J, T and the isolation weights are continuous), so the jump is only in the
choice of principal edge.

**Planner's look at levels (development data, disclosed).** On T1 the
detected rows sit on 707's lower branch: at 4595 MHz, measured 207.4 dB at
3 m and 204.7 dB at 9 m, against 707's 213.6 and 238.2 dB. So the branch that
counts more ridges is the wrong one here, and even the lower branch is 4-14 dB
pessimistic across 230-4595 MHz. This look rules out the "most-loss
decomposition" design below; it didn't choose a design that was then run.

| Design | Rule | Gate on the R2-020 sweeps (largest change between heights 0.05 m apart) | Why it fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Most-loss decomposition | L = max over principal edges p of L_casc(p) | Continuous by construction | Always takes the upper branch: +25 to +35 dB on T1's detected rows below the switch, the opposite of the measurements. Not run. |
| Least-loss of two ridges | L = min(L_casc(p1), L_casc(p2)), p2 the highest-nu ridge isolated from p1 | Continuous at the switch | Degenerate: P.526-11's T = 1 - exp(-J_p/6) shrinks every secondary when p is weak, so L_casc(p2) falls to a few dB whenever the rival ridge is low. It would remove the principal edge's loss on any path with a low second ridge. Not run. |
| J-weighted blend of the two | L = (J1 L_casc(p1) + J2 L_casc(p2)) / (J1 + J2) | T1 0.25-0.28 dB (pass); **T2 14.2 dB at 4595 MHz and 15.7 dB at 9190 MHz (fail)** | The rival ridge's identity changes at T2's second switch (25.75 m), so the jump moves rather than goes. The weights sit near 0.5 on both paths, which also lifts T1's low heights by 7-15 dB over 707. Prototyped, then removed from the code. |

**Decision.** No parameter-free, continuous selector built from P.526-11's
own pieces was found. A continuous selector needs either a published method
that is continuous by construction (P.526-16 section 4.5 Bullington is, and
fails the censored gate at 55 %, run 703) or a width parameter for the blend
(it would have to buy 0.5 dB, rule 7). The principal-edge jump stays a
documented defect of 707. A next attempt needs new design work: for example,
Vogler's coupled loss over a continuously weighted ridge set. That is a planning
task, not a quick candidate.
