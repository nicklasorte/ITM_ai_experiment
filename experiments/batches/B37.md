   (`diffraction_bound = "itm_troposcatter_line"`). 707's terminal clutter

| --- | --- | --- |

Checks: C3 must equal `var_lb_50` in

**Recorded per row:** loss for each arm; ITM's mode (line of sight,
diffraction, scatter) and whether the delta applies (transhorizon only,
`diffraction_region = "transhorizon"`); 707's parts L_ba, L_bs, A_s,
A_s − L_bs, bound_binds.

**Statistics** (mean over rows, 95 % interval over paths, 2,000 draws,
seed 20261137, numpy default_rng, paths sorted by `path_id`, draws of shape
(2000, 29)):

- Steps: Δ1 = C1 − S, Δ2 = C2 − C1, Δ3 = C3 − C2. Δ1 + Δ2 + Δ3 = C3 − S
  by construction; this identity is a check, not evidence.
- By ITM mode, and by whether the delta applies.
- On delta rows: mean L_ba − L_bs and mean max(A_s − L_bs, 0), and the
  share of rows where max(A_s − L_bs, 0) > 0.
- C4 − C3: does B25's spherical-earth reference change the R1 excess?

**Reading rule (decided before the run):** a step "carries" the excess if
its mean is at least half of mean(C3 − S) and its interval excludes zero.
If no step does, the result is "spread", and no single switch is a lead.

### Correction to section 3 (found in the run, before any analysis)

Section 3 says the delta construction applies on transhorizon rows only
(`diffraction_region = "transhorizon"`). That's wrong: `diffraction_region`
gates only the non-delta profile methods. In 707, the delta construction
replaces ITM's reference attenuation on every row, line of sight included
(`itm_p2p_core_exp.m`, lines 104–120). The by-mode breakdown below
replaces the "delta applies" breakdown. The rule and the statistics are
unchanged.

### D37b: attribution (model only; no measured value read)

**Reading (registered rule): Δ2 carries the excess on R1.** The cascade,

Descriptive results (`attribution_descriptive_rev1.csv`; plain means, no
intervals):

- **R1 by ITM mode.** On the 206 rows that stock ITM puts in its
  diffraction region, Δ2 is +9.46 dB (total +10.68 dB). On the 1,221
  line-of-sight rows, Δ2 is +0.95 dB (total +1.17 dB). The bound binds on
  24 % of the diffraction rows and on none of the line-of-sight rows.
- **R1 by frequency.** Δ2 is about the same at every frequency (+2.00,
  +2.12, +2.22 and +2.42 dB at 230, 410, 751 and 910 MHz). Δ1 falls with
  frequency (+2.02, +1.11, −0.49 and −0.69 dB).
- **R1 delta parts (707).** The mean of max(A_s − L_bs, 0) is 1.72 dB.
  A_s − L_bs is positive on 42 % of rows.
- **VA (243 rows on 61 paths; descriptive).** 707 adds +3.13 dB over stock
  (+2.40 to +3.91). Δ1 is +1.54 and Δ2 is +1.59, and the rule also reads
  Δ2 as the carrier there. In B36, though, the added loss moves VA toward
  the measurements (mean signed error: 707 −0.20 dB, stock −3.33 dB). At
  R1 it moves away from them (707 +3.17 dB, stock +0.62 dB). These signed
  errors are B36's numbers on the same rows; D37b read no measured value.
  The frequency labels in the CSV are rounded down (75 is 76 MHz, 172 is
  173 MHz).

What this means for improving ITM (planner's reading, not part of the
rule): the lead from B36 narrows to one part of 707. The cascade's extra
loss is largest on R1's diffraction-mode rows. The same change helps at
Virginia, so removing the cascade isn't supported. Any repair would have
to explain why the cascade over-counts on R1's gentle paths and not on
VA's (for example, through how secondary edges are found and weighted on
R1's profiles). That needs its own registration under the acceptance
rule, scored on all sites.

Notes:

- **T7.** Two of the eight cases (transmitter 2 m at 20 km, both
  frequencies) have no receiver height above −20 dB. Under the pass
  condition they're uninformative. Over all heights, their errors are
  0.002 and 0.000 dB.
- **T8 is a method failure, not a code bug.** The terrain-following
  transformation and its slope phase factors are derived from the
  narrow-angle equation. Pairing them with the exact free-space operator
  doesn't make the terrain method wide-angle, and on the 10° plane it's
  less accurate than the narrow operator. So the registered R-wide screen
  (narrow against wide) can't detect narrow-angle error. Fixing this would
  need a different method, such as a terrain transformation derived for
  the wide-angle operator, not another pass at this code. Under section 2,
  D37a stops here.
- **One finding from T8 itself** (one geometry, not a screen): the narrow
  operator's error on a uniform 10° plane at 410 MHz is 0.48 dB (median
  0.38 dB), against 0.16 dB at 5.7°. This bounds the narrow-angle error on
  one test geometry. It says nothing about the B36 rows.
