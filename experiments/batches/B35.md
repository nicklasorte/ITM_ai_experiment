## 1. Why this batch

`data/raw/its_phase2.zip` holds a fifth Phase 2 site that the builder never
read: R4, one receiver in a grove of cottonwood trees about 18 m high
(McQuate, Harman and McClanahan, OT/TRER 19, 1971). The search never used
it and it isn't a Tier B set. It allows three checks the development data
can't give:

1. Is 707 closer than stock ITM to the measured level at a site it wasn't
   chosen on?
2. Is the size of 707's kept terminal term (ITU-R P.2108-1 section 3.1)
   right for a terminal inside this canopy, measured as a contrast on one
   path?
3. How much does the in-canopy loss change between the leaves-on and
   leaves-off visits?

**Limits fixed before the run:**

- **One receiver, one grove.** The 36 paths share the receiver, so the
  intervals below describe spread over transmitter sites only. For any
  statement about wooded terminals in general, n is 1.
- **Not independent of the development data.** R4 is 7.4 km from R1, with
  the same equipment and years, and one transmitter position is also an R1
  position.
- **Blinding is by procedure, not by access.** The measured values sit in
  the committed archive. The planner has read `phase_2.txt`, `r4pars.txt`,
  `r4path.txt`, the header of `r4data.txt`, and its path, season and
  height columns, and no measured value. The red team worked under the
  same rule. The worker's scripts must not print a measured value before
  step 6.

## 2. Data and inputs

- **Measurements:** `phase_2/r4data.txt` in `data/raw/its_phase2.zip`
  (read-only). Nine fields on every line: Path, Season (S = leaves on,
  W = leaves off), Rht (receiver height, 1 to 24 m), then attenuation
  relative to free space at 230, 410, 910, 1846, 4595 and 9190 MHz. 36
  paths × 2 seasons × 24 heights = 1,728 lines. Codes, as
  `data/build_links_rev1.m` lines 169 to 171 read the other sites: 999 or
  more is missing; 500 up to 999 is a lower bound (value − 500), called
  censored here.
- **Geometry:** `r4path.txt` (36 paths, terminal coordinates, distance).
  The transmitter is the mobile end; the receiver is fixed in the grove.
- **System:** `r4pars.txt`: transmitter height 6.6 m (230, 410 MHz) or
  7.3 m (910 MHz and up). The 9190 MHz row appears twice; check the two
  rows are equal and use the first, as `build_links_rev1.m` line 153 does.
  Horizontal polarization (OT/TRER 19 section 2).
- **Terrain:** the archive has no profile files for R4. Sample one profile
  per path with `itm_terrain_profile_rev1` at 30 m, default source,
  transmitter first, as FRAMEWORK.md does for the long-term paths. These
  are bare-earth profiles from a modern source for 1966 paths; the grove
  isn't in them.
- **Model inputs, the same for every arm:** `eval/settings_rev1.m`
  (relative permittivity 25, conductivity 0.02 S/m, mode of variability
  13, 50 % time, location and confidence); polarization horizontal;
  climate and N_0 at the path midpoint as `build_links_rev1.m` lines 137
  to 139.
- **Loss convention:** as `build_links_rev1.m` line 172: L_fs = 32.45 +
  20 log10(f_MHz) + 20 log10(d_km) with d from `r4path.txt`; model
  attenuation A_model = L_model − L_fs with the same d.
- **Arms:**
  - **Stock:** `itm_p2p_rev1` at the pinned matlab_itm.
  - **707-O:** `itm_p2p_core_exp` with 707's switches (the override string
    in `b28_calibrate_rev1.m`) and
    `cfg.row = struct('set', "R4", 'siting', "O", 'reversed', false, 'path_id', <path>)`.
    The terminal term is off.
  - **707-C:** the same with `'siting', "C", 'reversed', true`. In
    `clutter_terminal` (lines 1533 to 1582), a set other than VA puts the
    term at end 1 and `reversed` moves it to end 2, the receiver. The term
    is 707's as kept: P.2108-1 section 3.1, trees and forest,
    representative height 15 m, street width 27 m (the Recommendation's
    Table 2 default, inherited from 707, not a property of this grove),
    base loss at max(h, 15 m), held at its 3 GHz value above 3 GHz.
    Nothing is retuned to the 18 m grove.
  - **Parity arm:** `itm_p2p_core_exp` at `params_exp()` defaults must
    equal stock on every row.

## 3. Registered rule

**Planner-chosen values (none comes from a source):** the height groups; the 1 dB size in "resolved" and in the E1 reading
rule; the 2 % length check; the 20-path floor; the 20 % drop threshold;
the 60° sector width; 2,000 draws; the 0.01 dB tie size.

**Height groups**, set from OT/TRER 19's 18 m tree height and 707's 15 m
representative height: **in canopy** 2 to 12 m (11 heights); **top of
tower** 21 to 24 m (4 heights, 3 to 6 m above the tree tops, so still
inside the first Fresnel zone of the grove at the lower frequencies; it is
not a tree-free reference). Heights 1 m and 13 to 20 m are reported and
are in no endpoint.

**Group value** for a path, season and frequency: the median over the
group's heights of A, with a censored cell counted as larger than every
uncensored cell. It exists when no height in the group is missing, the median falls on
uncensored cells (for the top-of-tower group, the mean of the two middle
cells), and no median cell exceeds the smallest censored bound in the
group (a bound is the largest observable loss less 5 dB, `phase_2.txt`,
so a censored cell isn't always above every uncensored one). The model's group value is the median over the same
heights. So censoring changes a group value only when half or more of a
group's cells are censored, and then the group is dropped and counted in
`support_rev1.csv`. Groups dropped for a missing height and groups dropped for censoring
are counted separately. If all dropped groups exceed 20 % of an
endpoint's cells, report that endpoint as descriptive with no outcome label, and say that
censoring drops remove the highest-loss cases. Cells of a frequency left
out by the 20-path floor don't count in this share.

**Cells:** one cell is a path × frequency × season. Error is model minus
measured. A model tie (two arms equal) counts as zero difference.

**Resampling:** 2,000 bootstrap draws over the 36 paths, all cells of a
path kept together. Deciding numbers: `numpy.random.default_rng(20261126)`,
used for pooled D1 and then pooled D2, in that order, and nothing else.
Paths are sorted by name; each draw is `rng.integers(0, 36, size=(2000, 36))`.
Every descriptive interval uses `default_rng(20261127)`, in the order E1
by frequency ascending, E2 splits as listed, then E3 by frequency
ascending (in canopy, top of tower, difference). 95 % percentile intervals. "Resolved" means the mean is
at least 1 dB in size and the interval excludes zero.

- **E1, level at the top of the tower, leaves off (primary).** Cells:
  top-of-tower group values in season W, all six frequencies (leaves off
  is the state with the least tree loss that neither arm models).
  D1 = mean |e_707-O| − mean |e_stock|.
  - Outcome A: D1 resolved and negative. Outcome B: D1 resolved and
    positive. Outcome C: anything else; C isn't equivalence.
  - **Reading rule, fixed now:** let m_s and m_7 be the mean signed errors
    of stock and 707-O over E1's cells. If m_s is below −1 dB (stock
    under-predicts) and m_7 is above m_s (707 predicts more loss), outcome
    A is reported as "707 closer by added loss where stock
    under-predicts", as B32 reported its VHF level result, and isn't
    credited to 707's change. In every other case A is read as support
    for 707's change at this site. The same two numbers are reported for
    each frequency, so opposite signs by band are visible.
  - A frequency under the 20-path floor is left out of pooled D1 and
    reported on its own.
  - Also reported: D1 by frequency; season S; mean signed error per arm;
    the share of cells where the arms differ by less than 0.01 dB; the
    propagation mode counts.
- **E2, size of the terminal term.** K = A(in canopy) − A(top of tower),
  per cell; e = K_model − K_measured. D2 = mean |e_707-C| − mean |e_707-O|
  on 230 to 1846 MHz, both seasons (P.2108 section 3.1's validated range
  ends at 3 GHz). "Kept term better than ITM's own height gain": D2 resolved and negative.
  "Worse": D2 resolved and positive. Otherwise no clear effect.
  - **What E2 can and can't show:** K is a median over heights, so E2
    tests the size of the term by frequency, not its shape with height. A
    constant loss of the same size would score the same; no control arm
    can separate them on K. The shape is reported separately: the
    least-squares slope of A against height over 2 to 12 m, measured and
    for each arm, descriptive.
  - **What the two arms are, in the code** (lines 89 to 94 and 208 to
    212): below 15 m, 707-C computes the path with the receiver at 15 m
    and adds the P.2108 term for the actual height; 707-O computes the
    path at the actual height. So D2 compares P.2108's height gain below
    15 m with ITM's own height gain below 15 m (both on 707's
    diffraction), not a term against no term. Between 15 m and the top of
    the tower both arms use ITM's height gain.
  - Reported, not deciding: 4595 and 9190 MHz (horn antennas of about
    12.5° beamwidth, OT/TRER 19 Table 1, as the red team read it); each
    season; the 12 paths that bear 205° to 265° from the receiver (within
    30° of the report's direction of greatest tree depth, 235°) against
    the other 24.
- **E3, change between visits.** S = A(leaves on) − A(leaves off), per
  path and frequency, for each group. Every arm predicts S = 0 by
  construction. The visits are months apart, so S holds leaf state and
  everything else that changed (ground, weather, equipment state).
  Reported: mean S per frequency and group, and the difference
  S(in canopy) − S(top of tower), which removes changes common to both
  heights. No outcome label.
- **For scale only:** ITU-R P.833-10 eq. (2) A_m with the St. Petersburg
  constants (A_1 = 1.37 dB, α = 0.42; 105.9 to 2117.5 MHz), listed beside
  the measured K. It isn't a limit or a test: those constants are a fit
  for a 1.5 m antenna in another forest, A_m also depends on species,
  density and antenna pattern, and K isn't excess over all other
  mechanisms.

Multiplicity: E1 and E2 each have one deciding number. Everything else is
descriptive.

A result here doesn't change `best`, k or any score.

## 4. Predictions (planner, before any measured value is read)

- E1: outcome C 50 %, A 35 %, B 15 %. If A, "by added loss" 60 %. Reason:
  most paths are short enough to be near line of sight from 22 m, where
  707 differs from stock ITM mainly through its smooth-surface change, and
  the grove still cuts the Fresnel zone, so both arms should under-predict.
- E2: D2 resolved and negative 45 %; resolved and positive 20 %.
- E3: S(in canopy) − S(top of tower) is positive at 910 MHz and above
  (65 %), and larger at 9190 than at 230 MHz (60 %).

## 5. Steps and checks (worker: follow exactly; hand back on any error or ambiguity)

Files to write: `experiments/batches/d35_build_rev1.m`,
`experiments/batches/d35_analysis_rev1.py`, `experiments/D35-r4-grove/`
(outputs). Use `d32_run_models_rev1.m` as the template for the model
script. Don't edit `eval/`, `data/` or `itm_variant/`.

1. **Keys without values.** `d35_build_rev1(root)` reads `r4pars.txt`,
   `r4path.txt` and `r4data.txt`. Check: 36 paths in both files with the
   same names; 1,728 data lines of nine fields; seasons S and W, 864 each;
   heights 1 to 24, 72 lines each; the two 9190 MHz rows of `r4pars.txt`
   equal. Stop if any check fails. The script prints counts only.
2. **Smoke trial.** `d35_build_rev1(root, Smoke=true)` runs one path
   (`R4-003-T1`), all 24 heights, six frequencies and four arms, and
   writes nothing. Check: no error; parity arm equals stock within
   1e-9 dB; 707-C equals 707-O at heights of 15 m and above, and differs
   below 15 m at 230 MHz.
3. **Full model run.** Writes `predictions_rev1.csv` (path, f_mhz,
   rx_ant_m, htx_m, d_km, profile_km, climate, N_0, the four arms' A, 707's
   mode) and `paths_rev1.csv`. Checks: every profile length within 2 % of
   the `r4path.txt` distance; parity within 1e-9 dB on every row; no
   non-finite loss. Stop and hand back if a check fails; don't widen a
   tolerance. Profiles stay in memory for the run (B33 found ITM jumps
   under CSV rounding).
4. **Commit and push the predictions** before step 6, so the model
   outputs are fixed before any measured value is analyzed.
5. **Analysis self-test.** `d35_analysis_rev1.py --selftest` on synthetic
   data: recovers planted outcomes A, B and C for E1 and the "by added
   loss" label; a planted right-size and a planted oversize term for E2; a
   planted visit shift for E3; a group with half its cells censored is
   dropped; a group with one censored cell keeps its median; a group with a missing
   height is dropped; a group whose median cell exceeds a censored bound is
   dropped.
6. **Analysis.** `d35_analysis_rev1.py <repo> experiments/D35-r4-grove`
   joins measurements to predictions by path, frequency, season and
   height, and writes `e1_rev1.csv`, `e2_rev1.csv`, `e3_rev1.csv`,
   `slopes_rev1.csv`, `support_rev1.csv` (cells, missing, censored and
   dropped counts by group, frequency and season) and
   `decision_rev1.json`.
7. **Results.** Fill section 6: each table with a Population column;
   predictions against outcomes; results that hold by construction
   labeled; "Not done" listed.
8. **Fresh reviewer** reruns the analysis and compares each claim
   sentence with its data. Then LOG entry, `batch_stability.tsv` row,
   guard, commit with the worker trailer, push.

Stop conditions: any count, parity or length check fails; fewer than 20
paths have a top-of-tower group value at a frequency in E1 (report that
frequency as descriptive and go on; hand back if it happens at more than
two frequencies); any step needs to write a file other than the two scripts, the
`experiments/D35-r4-grove/` outputs, this file, `experiments/LOG.md` and
`results/batch_stability.tsv`.
