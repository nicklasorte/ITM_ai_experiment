# Batch B06: second ridges that grow from zero, and a troposcatter bound

Planner: Claude Fable 5.2, 2026-11-21. Worker: the session model.
Predictions were committed before any scored run of these settings.

## Purpose

B05 found two things. Restoring ITM's clutter term A_fo in the delta
construction (502) gives the best composite so far (11.1, battery pass) but
predicts signal on 47 % of the no-signal rows. Counting ridges removes most
of that optimism on multi-ridge paths (501, 503, 504), but every multi-edge
setting failed the battery and two of them added hundreds of dB on long
paths. This batch builds a multi-edge term that passes the battery and is
bounded, puts it on 502, and runs the controls that separate the pieces.

## What 501's blow-up was (task 1)

Unbounded stacking of grazing edges, not a code defect. On the worst row's
path (LT_LT1804, 1,028 km, 417 MHz, 34,278 samples) the methods give:

| Method | Loss (dB) | Edges |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bullington | 83.4 | 1 |
| Deygout | 122.8 | 3 |
| P.526-11 cascaded | 173.9 | 3 |
| `string_ep` | 471.9 | 56 |

The stretched string touches 56 obstructions on the way over the Earth's
bulge. 40 of them have a knife-edge loss under 8 dB (median ν = 0.14): they
graze the string, and each costs the 6 dB of a grazing knife edge, so
56 edges sum to 472 dB. The code does what its header says. The physics is
wrong for this case: a row of grazing edges on a convex surface is the
smooth-Earth problem, and summing 6 dB per edge has no upper limit. (For N
equal, evenly spaced grazing edges the multiple-knife-edge solution gives
about 20 log10(N + 1) dB, not 6 N; this is the planner's recollection of
Vogler, Radio Science 17(6), 1982, and isn't used in any code: TODO, check
the paper before relying on it.)

A second cause applies to every delta form: the form L_a + max(A_s − L_s, 0)
has no troposcatter limit on L_a. ITM's A_s contains troposcatter, but once
L_a exceeds it, the result is L_a alone. On LT_LT1804 stock ITM is 107 dB
above free space (scatter) and the cascaded L_a alone is 174 dB.

### `"p526_cascaded_isolated"` (in `multi_edge_diffraction_rev1.m`)

ITU-R P.526-11 section 4.4.2 (principal edge, one secondary edge on each
side, L = J_p + T (J_t + J_r + C), C = 10 + 0.04 D, T = 1 − exp(−J_p/6),
eq. (49)–(52)), with each secondary edge weighted by how isolated it is from
the principal edge:

- For a candidate point k on one side of the principal edge p, c_k is the
  depth of the deepest point between k and p below k. Heights are measured
  from the line joining the antennas, Earth bulge included.
- w_k = min(1, c_k / (0.6 r1)), where r1 is the first Fresnel zone radius at
  that deepest point on the sub-path from k to p (ITU-R P.526-16 eq. (2)).
  0.6 of the first Fresnel zone is the clearance that makes two obstacles
  isolated in ITU-R P.526-16 section 2.5. The linear ramp from 0 to that
  clearance is a design choice; it adds no constant.
- The secondary edge on each side is the point with the largest w_k J(ν_k),
  and it contributes w_k J(ν_k). ν_k is Deygout's (P.526-11 eq. (49)).

Why this passes where 503 and 504 failed. The planner traced their battery
steps to two artifacts, both from points with no valley before the principal
edge:

1. The sample next to a top that falls between two samples is a second
   "edge" with ν of 2.4 on the ridge sweep at 2.5 km (20 dB). One sample
   later it's gone. That is the 18.5 dB step.
1. Ground next to the principal edge has ν just under 0 on its sub-path, so
   plain Deygout adds about 6 dB on each side the moment the principal edge
   appears. That is the 11.9 dB step on flat terrain (and the reason eq.
   (52) exists).

Such points now have weight 0. A second ridge's loss grows from zero as its
valley opens (tested: no step as a saddle deepens from 0 to 30 m). At most
three edges are counted, as in the Recommendation, so the sum is bounded in
the number of edges. On a smooth Earth the weights are 0. The candidate
named in the planner prompt (starting J at ν = −0.78) is already how eq.
(31) is applied; the steps came from the two artifacts above, not from J.

### `cfg.diffraction_bound` (in `itm_p2p_core_exp.m`)

An upper bound on the delta forms. Beyond the horizon the troposcatter field
is present whatever the terrain does to the diffracted field.

- `"itm_troposcatter_line"`: on every transhorizon path the loss can't
  exceed ITM v1.4's troposcatter line through (d_5, A_5) and (d_6, A_6),
  evaluated at d. ITM itself uses that line only beyond d_x. Using it at
  shorter transhorizon distances as an upper bound is a design choice.
- `"itm_troposcatter"`: the bound applies only where ITM puts the actual
  path in its troposcatter region. Built first; it steps 15 dB on the
  frequency sweep where ITM changes region, so it's not scheduled.

### Delta forms

`"delta_cascaded_isolated"` and `"delta_cascaded_isolated_afo"`: the form
L_a + max(A_s − L_s, 0) (ITU-R P.452-18 eq. (38)–(40)) with
`"p526_cascaded_isolated"` on the actual and the zero-height profile; `_afo`
adds ITM's A_fo as in 502.

## Reviewer pass on 502 (task 4): yes, in parallel, and before any keep

A_fo is ITM's allowance for terrain irregularity near the antennas
(5 log10(1 + 10⁻⁵ h_1 h_2 f σ_h), at most 15 dB). In the delta forms, L_a
already takes the obstruction loss from the actual profile, so A_fo may
count the same terrain twice. 502's gain doesn't settle that: a loss of up
to 15 dB on every irregular path would also help if it stood in for
something else (rounded tops, clutter, the eq. (57) correction being too
small).

Decision: the batch can run without waiting, because no result is merged
without the pass. **If any `_afo` run passes every gate, the worker doesn't
merge it: record "passes gates; held for the reviewer pass" and hand back.**

Reviewer brief (three independent fresh agents; act on findings two of
three agree on). Read `itm_variant/itm_p2p_core_exp.m` (`diffraction_loss`,
`delta_bullington_reference`), ITM v1.4's DiffractionLoss, experiments 401
and 502 in `experiments/LOG.md`, and
`experiments/502-delta-bullington-afo/predictions_*.csv`. Questions:

1. In ITM v1.4, what does A_fo represent, and from which data was it set?
   Cite the public source (NTIA Report 82-100; Hufford's algorithm memo).
1. In L_ba + max(A_s − L_bs, 0) + A_fo, is any loss counted twice? Consider
   the Bullington eq. (57) correction, L_ba itself, and ITM's own use of
   A_fo only with terrain irregularity above zero.
1. Is 502's gain over 401 spread over paths in proportion to A_fo, or

   isn't shadowed; A_fo only where L_ba is under 6 dB.)
