## 1. Question

B23 found that 707 gives too little loss reduction when the fixed antenna
rises (R1 open blocked-blocked height pairs, 149 pairs, 29 bases: contrast
error +5.64 dB [3.81, 7.49]). Stock ITM's diffraction terms depend on antenna
height, so it's the nearest available proxy for M1. B23 reported only that
stock's |e| is larger than 707's, not its sign.

Does the measured contrast lie between 707's and stock's? If it does, a
sourced height-gain term could help, and stage B is worth a planner's time. If
it doesn't, M1 closes before anyone writes model code.

## 4. Registered rule

Decided on one population: **R1 open blocked-blocked detected height pairs**
(strict tier). Other cells are reported, labeled exploratory, and don't
decide.

| Outcome | Condition | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Signed e_707 and e_stock have opposite signs, both 95 % intervals exclude zero, and the in-sample blend ceiling cuts the equal-base mean \|e\| by at least 1 dB, on at least 10 bases | Hand to a planner for stage B |
| B | Opposite signs as in A, but the ceiling is under 1 dB | Close M1 |
| C | Anything else (same sign, an interval that includes zero, or fewer than 10 bases) | Hand back to the planner with the table. Don't interpret. |

The 10-base minimum and the 1 dB step reuse the planner-chosen values in
B23.md section 3.4 (there: a minimum cluster count and a planning
resolution). They have no external source. Here they decide only whether a
planner spends time on stage B; they never enter a model.

Planner's predictions, before the run:
- e_stock on the deciding population is negative (stock overshoots): 60 %.
- Outcome A 40 %, B 15 %, C 45 %.

What the result can't show:
- The blend ceiling is optimistic twice: its weight is chosen in sample, and
  blending averages the two models' independent errors. Outcome A means
  "worth a model-only test", not "M1 works".
- At weight 0 the blend is 707, so the in-sample cut can't be negative. That
  holds by construction and isn't evidence.
- The deciding population has one fixed receiver. Its 29 bases aren't
  independent sites.
- Pairs with a censored row are left out, so the high-loss rows are missing
  (B23 review).
