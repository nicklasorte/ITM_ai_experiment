## Question

707's delta construction is L_ba + max(A_s − L_bs, 0). A_s is ITM run on the smooth path, which
stands in for the spherical-earth diffraction loss. On line-of-sight smooth paths, ITM puts all
weight on its two-ray term, so A_s lobes with height (B20 D20a: A_s rises 3.7 dB over the
R1-005-T5 sweep, with steps up to 1.33 dB). The published construction uses a spherical-earth
diffraction kernel with no two-ray term.

Does replacing A_s with that kernel, and changing nothing else, remove the lobing, and how much
does it move predictions?

## Source

- ITU-R P.452-18 §4.2.2 eq. (23)–(37): spherical-earth diffraction loss L_dsph, with the land
  ground constants of eq. (29) (ε_r = 22, σ = 0.003 S/m). §4.2.3 eq. (38)–(40) place it as
  L_ba + max(L_dsph − L_bs, 0), the same placement 707 already uses.
- ITU-R P.526-16 §3.2 gives the same procedure; §4.5.2 eq. (66) the positive-part form.
- Independent check: Nick's `matlab_itu_p452` port (`private/p452_core.m`, `spherical_earth`),
  which its `test_p452_oracle_rev1` matches to 1e-9 dB against the ITU-R reference code
  (eeveetza/p452, commit a719515).

## Change (off by default)

New option `cfg.smooth_reference`:
- `"itm"`: A_s as now (default; 707 unchanged).
- `"p452_spherical"`: L_dsph from P.452-18 §4.2.2 on the smooth path, with the antenna heights
  above the smooth surface (eq. (156)), d in km, the effective earth radius 1/γ_e and the row's
  polarization.

Everything else stays: surface, cascade, bound, clutter, variability. The new function is
`itm_variant/spherical_earth_diffraction_rev1.m`.

Known limit: P.452's fixed land constants replace ITM's ground impedance in this one term. That is
the published construction, not a fit.

**Disposition:** closed under the registered rule (the screen rules it out, and the lobing isn't
removed). The option stays in the code, off by default; 707 is unchanged.

**Finding, on one row: the R1-005-T5 inversion at 230 MHz is not two-ray lobing.** A kernel with no
two-ray term rises the same way on this sweep, with its largest step at the same place (13 to 14 m)
and the same peak (15 m). The likely carrier is the surface (`surface_tx_rev1.csv`): as the
transmitter rises from 1 to 15 m, the receiver's height above the obstruction-adjusted surface
(P.452-18 eq. (151)-(153)) falls steadily from 4.59 m to its 1.0 m floor, while L_dsph rises from
17.0 to 25.7 dB; after the floor is reached, L_dsph falls. Step by step the link is weak
(correlation 0.18 over 1 to 15 m), so the surface explains the trend, not each step. This corrects
B20 D20a's attribution for this row. Whether other inversions share the cause is untested.
